---
title: A date is not yet a government
dek: Some calendars list an election while the state is still under a transitional arrangement.
---

South Sudan has been governed by a transitional arrangement under peace agreements, not by a settled cycle of elected terms. International election calendars list a general election for 22 December 2026, covering the presidency, the Transitional National Legislative Assembly, and the Council of States. This desk marks that date tentative. Earlier announced dates were postponed.

A line on a calendar answers a narrow question: someone has published a day. It does not answer whether the vote will be held, or whether it will be free and fair. Those claims need their own sources. Until a dated change of regime is written into the regime file, with a source, this desk does not announce one.

The contrast is a country with a boring clock. New Zealand's parliament lasts three years. Germany's Bundestag lasts four. The existence of a date, in those cases, sits on top of an office that already has a term. In a transitional arrangement, the date is the thing still being tested.
