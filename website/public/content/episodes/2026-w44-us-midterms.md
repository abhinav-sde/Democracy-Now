## Open

Tuesday 3 November 2026 is election day in the United States. It is a midterm. That word only means the election falls halfway through a presidential term. The president is not a candidate.

Three different kinds of office share the day. Voters elect the entire House of Representatives, part of the Senate, and governors in 36 states. The first two are the federal legislature. Governors are the executives of their states. They are not members of the president's cabinet, and they are not elected as a national slate.

## On the ballot

The House has 435 voting seats, and every one of them is up. A representative's term is two years, so the House is always elected in full. The Congress sworn in on 3 January 2027 is the 120th.

The Senate has 100 seats and six-year terms. They are staggered, so a regular election replaces about a third of the chamber. This year that is 33 Class II seats, last elected in 2020, plus two special elections: Florida, for the seat Marco Rubio left, and Ohio, for the seat J.D. Vance left. Thirty-five Senate seats in all.

Governors are up in 36 states. A governor runs the state government. The count of 36 is states only. Territorial contests are not given their own rows on this desk.

New Zealand is four days later, on 7 November, electing its parliament under a three-year term. That is a national parliamentary election, not a midterm, and it is the comparison for the lesson below.

## How this government works

The United States separates the election of the legislature from the election of the president. Congress can change hands while the president stays in office. A midterm is how that happens. It does not choose a prime minister, because there isn't one. The president is both head of state and head of government, and this ballot does not pick that office.

A parliamentary country uses a national election differently. In the United Kingdom, India, or New Zealand, voters elect a parliament, and the government is whoever can command that parliament. Lose the parliament and the government goes. The United States does not link the two that way. The House term is two years, the Senate term is six, and the presidential term is four. They are meant to expire on different days.

The governors on this ballot are a third clock. They belong to state constitutions. Sharing 3 November with Congress is a fact of the calendar, not a sign that a governor sits in the federal government.

None of this says whether the election will be close, or free and fair, or likely to change control of Congress. Those are arguments for after the votes, made with results this desk does not invent in advance.
