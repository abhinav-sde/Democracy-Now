## Open

The first weekend of October is a national election weekend in three countries, and the three ballots do not choose a government in the same way.

On Saturday 3 October, Latvia elects the Saeima, its one-hundred-seat parliament. On Sunday 4 October, Bosnia and Herzegovina elects a three-member presidency and the House of Representatives. The same day, Brazil votes for president, all 513 seats in the Chamber of Deputies, and 54 of 81 Senate seats.

India's big state elections for 2026 are already over. Assam, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, West Bengal, and Puducherry voted in April. They sit on this desk as held. Results are not filled in until an Election Commission source is added.

## On the ballot

Latvia is the simplest of the three to describe. Voters elect the whole parliament for a four-year term. They do not elect the president. The Saeima chooses the president, and the government has to hold the confidence of that parliament.

Bosnia and Herzegovina votes for a presidency of three. One Bosniak and one Croat are elected in the Federation, and one Serb is elected in Republika Srpska. The same day, voters elect the 42-seat House of Representatives. The terms are four years.

Brazil is a general election. The president is elected for four years. The Chamber of Deputies is elected in full, also for four years. The Senate is different: a senator sits for eight years, so only some seats are up each time. In 2026 that is two-thirds of the Senate, 54 seats, two in each state and in the Federal District. There is no second round for the Senate. The two most-voted candidates in each state take the seats.

State governors are on the Brazilian ballot the same day. This brief stays with the national offices.

## How this government works

Brazil's presidency is a majority office. A winner needs more than half of the valid votes. If nobody has that on 4 October, the top two candidates meet again on 25 October. That second round is not listed on the calendar until it is required. It is a rule of the election, not a second country going to the polls.

That is a different machine from Latvia. In Latvia, the question this weekend is not who is president. There is no Latvian presidential vote. The question is who holds the Saeima. The government is then formed out of that result.

It is also different from the United States, which votes on 3 November and does not use a national second round. The lesson for this week is the Brazilian one: the first count can be the end of the presidential election, or only the end of the first half.

The Chamber and the Senate do not work that way. Deputies are chosen by proportional representation. Senators, this year, are the two leading candidates in each state. A presidential runoff does not reopen those races.

Israel elects the whole Knesset on 27 October.
