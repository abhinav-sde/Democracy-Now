# March 2026 — four weekends

Archive plans. No winners.

## Weekend of 7 March

- **Air:** Sunday 8 March, 120 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Colombia, congress, this Sunday. Nepal was Thursday.

### Cold open

Colombia elects a House of Representatives and a Senate today. It does not elect a president. That vote is 31 May, and it may need a second round on 21 June. Switzerland also votes today, on referendums, which do not elect the Federal Council. Nepal elected a House of Representatives on Thursday. We will describe these offices and we will not name a winner.

### On the desk

- 5 March — Nepal — House of Representatives — held Thursday
- 8 March — Colombia — House and Senate — today
- 8 March — Switzerland — referendums — today
- 3–8 March — Iran — Supreme Leader — indirect, one sentence
- 15 March — Kazakhstan, referendum; North Korea, parliament; Congo, president; Vietnam, parliament

### The ballot

Colombia’s split calendar. Draw it. March is the legislature. May is the president. A voter can change one and not the other. That is the sentence of the night.

### How this government works

Switzerland is the comparison, not a second congress. A referendum asks a question. The seven-member Federal Council is elected by parliament, not by this Sunday’s voters. People hear “Switzerland votes” and think the government is on the ballot. It is not.

### The other chair

Nepal. Parliamentary. The house was the ballot. The prime minister is a creature of that house. No winner.

### Third chair

Iran, one minute. The Supreme Leader was chosen inside an assembly this week. Not a popular vote. Then name the four lines on 15 March so the audience knows why next week is two hours.

### The month ahead

Slovenia’s parliament on 22 March. Italy’s constitutional referendum on 22 and 23 March. Denmark on 24 March. Faroe Islands on 26 March.

### What we will not say

- We will not say Colombia elected a president today.
- We will not say Switzerland elected a government today.
- We will not score Nepal.

### Watch next

Sunday 15 March, four countries, one date, four mechanisms.

## Weekend of 14 March

- **Air:** Sunday 15 March, 120 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** the 15 March cluster

### Cold open

Four lines on the calendar share this date. Kazakhstan is a constitutional referendum. North Korea is a parliamentary election in which the public is not choosing among competing governments. The Republic of the Congo is electing a president. Vietnam is electing a parliament in a one-party system. Same date. Not the same act. We will not grade any of them, and we will not name a winner.

### On the desk

- 15 March — Kazakhstan — constitutional referendum
- 15 March — North Korea — parliament
- 15 March — Republic of the Congo — president
- 15 March — Vietnam — parliament
- 16 March — India — Rajya Sabha, 37 seats — indirect
- 22 March — Slovenia — National Assembly
- 22–23 March — Italy — constitutional referendum

### The ballot

Give each of the four a clean minute on chooser and office. Referendum. Non-competitive parliament. President. One-party parliament. The host’s job is to stop the audience merging them into “Africa and Asia voted.”

### How this government works

The lesson is the word “election” on a calendar. It means a date and an office. It does not mean a competitive change of government, and it does not mean a referendum. This is the show that teaches the staff, as well as the audience, how the rest of the year will be spoken.

### The other chair

India’s Rajya Sabha tomorrow. State assemblies elect members of the Council of States. The public does not. Six-year terms, one third at a time. Thirty-seven seats, ten states. Indirect.

### Third chair

Slovenia next Sunday, a parliamentary election, and Italy’s two-day constitutional referendum starting the same Sunday. Preview only.

### The month ahead

Denmark’s snap election on 24 March is the last big popular vote of the month.

### What we will not say

- We will not rank these four countries.
- We will not call North Korea’s or Vietnam’s vote a change of governing party.
- We will not call Kazakhstan’s referendum a presidential election.
- We will not give the Congo a winner.

### Watch next

Sunday 22 March. Slovenia, and Italy’s referendum.

## Weekend of 21 March

- **Air:** Sunday 22 March, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Slovenia, National Assembly, this Sunday

### Cold open

Slovenia elects a National Assembly today. The government comes from that assembly. The president of Slovenia is not on this ballot; that office is due in 2027, and it has no day yet. Italy begins a two-day constitutional referendum today. It does not elect the Chamber or the Senate.

### On the desk

- 22 March — Slovenia — National Assembly — today
- 22–23 March — Italy — constitutional referendum
- 22 March — North Korea — president of the State Affairs Commission — indirect, one sentence
- 23 March — Laos — president — indirect, elected by the assembly chosen on 22 February
- 24 March — Denmark — parliament
- 26 March — Faroe Islands — parliament

### The ballot

Slovenia. Parliamentary. Members today, a government afterwards, inside the assembly. Do not skip the word “afterwards.”

### How this government works

Italy’s referendum is the comparison. A constitution can be amended by a popular yes-or-no without a single deputy being elected. The next Italian parliamentary election is not this weekend. It is undated in 2027.

### The other chair

Denmark on Tuesday. Snap election of the Folketing, 179 seats, a prime minister who comes from that chamber. The Faroes vote on Thursday for their own parliament. Promise both for next Sunday’s desk.

### What we will not say

- We will not name Slovenia’s next prime minister.
- We will not say Italy elected a parliament this weekend.
- We will not turn the indirect presidential votes in North Korea and Laos into popular elections.

### Watch next

Sunday 29 March, Denmark and the Faroes. India is two weeks away.

## Weekend of 28 March

- **Air:** Sunday 29 March, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Denmark, 24 March; Faroe Islands, 26 March

### Cold open

Denmark held a snap election of the Folketing on Tuesday. One hundred seventy-nine seats: 175 from Denmark, two from Greenland, two from the Faroe Islands. The prime minister is whoever can command that chamber. The Faroe Islands held their own parliamentary election on Thursday. We have no winner on the desk for either. Next month, India begins counting assemblies toward a vote on 9 April, and Hungary, Benin, and Peru share the weekend of 12 April.

### On the desk

- 24 March — Denmark — parliament — held
- 26 March — Faroe Islands — parliament — held
- 30 March — Myanmar — president — indirect
- 4 April — Maldives — constitutional referendum
- 9 April — India — Assam, Kerala, Puducherry assemblies

### The ballot

Use Denmark to finish the snap-election lesson started last month. A term is a ceiling. Calling an election is a decision by a prime minister, not a sign that the clock ran out.

### How this government works

The Faroes sit inside the Danish kingdom and still have a parliament of their own. A viewer who hears both dates and thinks it was one election has mis-heard the week. Say that.

### The other chair

India. Assam, 126 seats. Kerala, 140. Puducherry, 30, and Puducherry is a Union territory with a legislature. One phase each, 9 April. Counting was scheduled for 4 May. No result will be recorded even after counting, until a commission source is in the file. Preview, not a result.

### What we will not say

- We will not name a Danish prime minister.
- We will not merge the Faroese election into Denmark’s.
- We will not start India’s result early.

### Watch next

Sunday 5 April. Maldives referendum, and the last preview before the Indian assemblies.
