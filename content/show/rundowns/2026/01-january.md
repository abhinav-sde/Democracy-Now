# January 2026 — five weekends

Archive plans. No winners.

## Weekend of 3 January

- **Air:** Sunday 4 January, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Benin, parliament, 11 January, confirmed. Myanmar, parliament phase 2, the same day.

### Cold open

Next Sunday is not a presidential election in Benin. It is the National Assembly. The president of Benin is a different day, 12 April. On the same Sunday, Myanmar holds the second phase of a parliamentary vote. Those two lines share a date and do not share a system. This hour is how to tell them apart.

### On the desk

- 11 January — Benin — parliament — confirmed
- 11 January — Myanmar — parliament, phase 2 — confirmed as a published phase
- 15 January — Uganda — president and parliament — confirmed
- 18 January — Portugal — president, first round — confirmed

### The ballot

Benin. The public elects the assembly. The president is not a candidate. January does not choose the head of government by itself if the constitution keeps that office on the April ballot.

### How this government works

Two elections in one country are a design, not a delay. Benin splits the assembly and the president by three months. Uganda, on Thursday 15 January, does the opposite and elects both on one day. The comparison is the split, not which country is better.

### The other chair

Myanmar’s second phase. Say “phased parliamentary vote.” Do not describe it as an ordinary change of governing party. Phase three is 25 January. Do not close the Myanmar election this Sunday.

### What we will not say

- We will not name a winner in Benin or Myanmar.
- We will not score either election.
- We will not call 11 January a presidential election in Benin.

### Watch next

Sunday 11 January is election day for Benin and for Myanmar’s second phase. Uganda is the Thursday after that.

## Weekend of 10 January

- **Air:** Sunday 11 January, 120 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Benin and Myanmar, this Sunday

### Cold open

Today the polls are for Benin’s National Assembly, and for the second phase of Myanmar’s parliamentary vote. Neither result will be read on this show. On Thursday, Uganda votes for a president and a parliament on the same day. Next Sunday, Portugal begins a presidential election that may need 8 February.

### On the desk

- 11 January — Benin — parliament — today
- 11 January — Myanmar — phase 2 — today
- 15 January — Uganda — president and parliament
- 18 January — Portugal — president, first round
- 25 January — Myanmar — phase 3

### The ballot

Walk the Benin office again, slowly, because it is election day. Assembly now. President on 12 April. A viewer who hears only “Benin votes” will get the wrong office.

### How this government works

A general election is when the executive and the legislature are chosen together, the way Uganda does on Thursday. A legislative election is when only the chamber is chosen. The word “election” does not say which one you are watching.

### The other chair

Uganda on Thursday. One day, two offices. That is the preview, not a result.

### Third chair

Portugal. Semi-presidential. The public elects a president next Sunday. The prime minister is not on that ballot. If nobody wins a majority, the top two meet on 8 February.

### The month ahead

Myanmar’s third phase on 25 January. Do not treat phase two as the end.

### What we will not say

- We will not read a count from today.
- We will not call Myanmar’s phase the last vote.
- We will not call Portugal’s 18 January the election of a prime minister.

### Watch next

Sunday 18 January is Portugal’s first round, and the desk on Uganda.

## Weekend of 17 January

- **Air:** Sunday 18 January, 120 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Portugal, president, first round, this Sunday

### Cold open

Portugal votes for a president today. A majority wins it in this round. If nobody has a majority, there is a second round on 8 February, and only the top two go through. The prime minister is not on the ballot. The Assembly of the Republic is not on the ballot. Uganda voted on Thursday for a president and a parliament together. That was a different shape of day.

### On the desk

- 15 January — Uganda — president and parliament — held Thursday, no result on this desk
- 18 January — Portugal — president, first round — today
- 25 January — Myanmar — parliament, phase 3
- 1 February — Costa Rica — president and parliament

### The ballot

Portugal, first round. Say the majority rule. Say 8 February as conditional. Do not name a winner tonight even if a count is moving. This archive plan has no winner in the file.

### How this government works

Semi-presidential means two executives. The president is elected by the public. The prime minister has to have the assembly. Electing one does not automatically replace the other. France, in April 2027, will be the same lesson with more time to teach it. Plant that comparison in one sentence.

### The other chair

Uganda’s Thursday vote. President and parliament, one day. No result read.

### Third chair

Myanmar phase three, next Sunday’s date, 25 January. One minute of warning: the election is not over at phase two.

### The month ahead

Costa Rica on 1 February elects a president and a parliament together. That Sunday is the fifth weekend of January.

### What we will not say

- We will not call a first-round leader the president.
- We will not invent a Portuguese prime minister from this vote.
- We will not give Uganda a winner.

### Watch next

Sunday 25 January, Myanmar’s third phase. Then Costa Rica on 1 February.

## Weekend of 24 January

### Cold open

Myanmar’s parliamentary vote reaches its third published phase today. The desk does not grade it, and it does not pretend the three phases were an ordinary multi-party change of government. Portugal’s presidential race, if it needs a second round, is on 8 February. Costa Rica’s general election is next Sunday.

### On the desk

- 25 January — Myanmar — phase 3 — today
- 1 February — Costa Rica — president and parliament
- 8 February — Japan — House of Representatives
- 8 February — Portugal — presidential second round, only if required
- 8 February — Thailand — House and constitutional referendum

### The ballot

Explain a phased election in one pass: the country does not vote on one day, so a partial day is not a national result. Then stop. No color on the regime beyond “this is the published phase.”

### How this government works

Costa Rica next week is the contrast, and it is the structure lesson tonight. President and assembly, one Sunday, one country, one general election. A threshold can force a presidential runoff. The assembly does not have one. The correspondent reads the tribunal’s threshold from the card if it is on the desk; otherwise says “a threshold, and we will read the number from the tribunal next week.”

### The other chair

The 8 February cluster, named only: Japan’s lower house, a possible Portuguese runoff, Thailand’s house plus a referendum. Three acts, one Sunday, saved for the two-hour show.

### What we will not say

- We will not close Myanmar with a winner.
- We will not assume Portugal’s second round if the file does not say it was required. For this archive, the next show explains the second round as the calendar’s published runoff date without naming who won the first.

### Watch next

## Weekend of 31 January

- **Air:** Sunday 1 February, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Costa Rica, president and parliament, this Sunday

### Cold open

Costa Rica holds a general election today. The president and the Legislative Assembly are on the same ballot. A presidential candidate who misses the legal threshold faces a runoff. The assembly is finished in this round. We will not name a winner.

### On the desk

- 1 February — Costa Rica — president and parliament — today
- 6 February — Tokelau — parliament — one sentence next week
- 8 February — Japan, Portugal’s second round, Thailand’s house and referendum

### The ballot

Offices, same day, different finishing rules. Say that twice. It is the thing people mis-hear.

### How this government works

Compare with Benin, which refused to put the two offices on one day. Compare with Uganda, which did. Costa Rica is the third shape: same day, but the presidency can run longer than the assembly count if the threshold is missed.

### The other chair

A one-minute map of 8 February so the audience knows next week is a two-hour show.

### What we will not say

- We will not declare a Costa Rican president tonight.
- We will not give the assembly a runoff it does not have.

### Watch next
