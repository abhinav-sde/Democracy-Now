## Weekend of 11 July

- **Air:** Sunday 12 July, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** none. Structure: staggered upper houses.

### Cold open

Nothing national is on the calendar today. The next popular vote in our book is the president of São Tomé and Príncipe on 19 July. This hour is about chambers that are never fully renewed: a third at a time, or two thirds, or a class.

### On the desk

- 19 July — São Tomé and Príncipe — president
- 12 August — Cook Islands — parliament
- 13 August — Zambia — president and parliament

### The ballot

Say the empty week out loud. Then preview São Tomé in ten minutes: president now, parliament on 27 September.

### How this government works

The Czech Senate in October is one third of 81 seats, six-year terms, and it does not choose the government. The government comes from the Chamber of Deputies. The United States Senate in November is a class, plus two specials, and 65 senators stay home. Brazil’s Senate in October is 54 of 81 seats, eight-year terms. Three staggers, three countries, one idea: the upper house is built to outlast one election.

### The other chair

Zambia in August, as a glimpse only. President and parliament together, the opposite of Cape Verde’s split. Do not teach it fully. August has five weekends for that.

### What we will not say

- We will not invent a ballot for today.
- We will not call a senate election a change of government when the government does not come from that senate.

### Watch next

Sunday 19 July. São Tomé’s president.
