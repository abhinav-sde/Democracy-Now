The first three Sundays are archive plans. The Sunday of 27 September is the last show before the October cluster. Today on the calendar of this book is Wednesday 30 September, so that Sunday has just aired. No winners.

## Weekend of 5 September

- **Air:** Sunday 6 September, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Sweden, parliament, next Sunday

### Cold open

Sweden elects the Riksdag on 13 September. Three hundred forty-nine seats. A four-year term. The public does not elect the prime minister by name. The Riksdag does, by declining to vote a nominee down. Nothing else national is on this week’s desk.

### On the desk

- 13 September — Sweden — parliament
- 18 September — South Ossetia — president
- 18–20 September — Russia — State Duma
- 23 September — Morocco — House of Representatives

### The ballot

Sweden, taught as a preview so election Sunday can be slower. Threshold: 4 percent nationally, or 12 percent in a constituency. Negative parliamentarism: the nominee becomes prime minister unless a majority says no.

### How this government works

Compare with a presidential ballot the audience already knows from this year, Colombia or Peru. There, the public finishes a person. Here, the public finishes a chamber, and the chamber tolerates a person.

### The other chair

Russia’s Duma, three days, 18 to 20 September. The president of Russia is not on that ballot. South Ossetia’s presidential vote on the 18th is a separate line, in a territory whose status this show will not decide. Name both. Teach them on the 20th.

### What we will not say

- We will not predict Sweden’s prime minister.
- We will not call the Duma a presidential election.

### Watch next

Sunday 13 September. Sweden votes.
