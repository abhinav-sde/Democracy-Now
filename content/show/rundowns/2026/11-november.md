# November 2026 — four weekends

On the board. No winners.

## Weekend of 7 November

- **Air:** Sunday 8 November, 120 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** New Zealand, yesterday. United States, Tuesday.

### Cold open

New Zealand elected its House of Representatives yesterday. The term is three years. The public did not elect a president. There isn’t one to elect. On Tuesday the United States elected a House, 35 senators, and 36 governors. It did not elect a president. This show can explain how those results would be read. It cannot read them. They are not in our file.

### On the desk

- 3 November — United States — House, Senate, governors — held Tuesday
- 7 November — New Zealand — parliament — yesterday
- 15 November — Cape Verde — president
- 28 November — Palestine — parliament
- 29 November — Switzerland — referendums

### The ballot

New Zealand first, because it is fresher and simpler. A parliament. A three-year maximum. A government is the group that can command the house, asked to form by the governor-general. No presidential count is missing. There was never going to be one.

### How this government works

Then the United States, as a way of reading, not a result. Three different maps. The House can change as a whole. The Senate can barely change, because 65 members stayed. The governors are a third map, of states. A viewer who wants one national number is asking for an office that was not on the ballot.

### The other chair

Cape Verde next Sunday. The presidency, after a parliament on 17 May. The pair closes.

### Third chair

Palestine on 28 November, parliament. Switzerland the next day, referendums. Name them. Do not teach them yet.

### The month ahead

December opens with The Gambia’s president on the 5th and a tentative general election in Guinea-Bissau on the 6th.

### What we will not say

- We will not assign the House, the Senate, or a governor.
- We will not say the American president was re-elected or defeated.
- We will not name a New Zealand prime minister.

### Watch next

Sunday 15 November. Cape Verde.

## Weekend of 14 November

- **Air:** Sunday 15 November, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Cape Verde, president, this Sunday

### Cold open

Cape Verde elects a president today. It elected a parliament on 17 May. The assembly is not on today’s ballot. That split is the reason this Sunday exists as its own show. We will not name the president.

### On the desk

- 15 November — Cape Verde — president — today
- 28 November — Palestine — parliament
- 29 November — Switzerland — referendums
- 5 December — The Gambia — president
- 6 December — Guinea-Bissau — president and assembly — tentative

### The ballot

Office, day, and the May date written next to it on the paper calendar. Term from the commission card if it is on the desk. No guessed years.

### How this government works

Put Zambia beside it for three minutes. Zambia did both offices in August. Cape Verde refused to. São Tomé did president in July and parliament in September. Three African calendars, three designs, no ranking.

### The other chair

The Gambia on 5 December is a president, and its parliament is not until 10 April 2027. The split can be even wider than Cape Verde’s. Preview only.

### What we will not say

- We will not call today a general election.
- We will not call 6 December confirmed.

### Watch next

Sunday 22 November, a structure hour before Palestine. Sunday 29 November, Palestine and Switzerland.

Nothing national is on the calendar today. Next Saturday, 28 November, is a parliamentary election in Palestine. Next Sunday is a Swiss referendum day, the fourth of the year. December is already on the paper: The Gambia, a tentative Guinea-Bissau, Haiti, a tentative South Sudan.

### The ballot

Say the empty day. Then Palestine: the calendar line is a parliament. Do not add a presidential election the line does not contain.

### How this government works

Use the quiet Sunday to finish the staggered-senate lesson if October did not have room, or to explain a tentative date. Guinea-Bissau is the example. The international calendar prints 6 December. Our desk prints tentative. Both sentences are said. The host does not pick the more exciting one.

### The other chair

### What we will not say

- We will not firm up a tentative date because the month looks empty.
