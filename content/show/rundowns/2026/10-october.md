# October 2026 — five weekends

On the board. This is the month the show was built for. No winners. Brazil’s 25 October and the Czech second round are conditional.

## Weekend of 3 October

- **Air:** Sunday 4 October, 120 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Latvia Saturday; Bosnia and Brazil today

### Cold open

Three countries, two days. Yesterday Latvia elected the Saeima, one hundred seats, a parliament. The president of Latvia was not on that ballot. Today Bosnia and Herzegovina elects a three-member presidency and a House of Representatives of forty-two. Today Brazil elects a president, all 513 deputies, and 54 of 81 senators. A Brazilian president needs more than half the valid votes. If nobody has that, the top two meet on 25 October. That Sunday is not a fact yet. We will not name a winner in any of the three.

### On the desk

- 3 October — Latvia — Saeima — yesterday
- 4 October — Bosnia and Herzegovina — presidency and House of Representatives — today
- 4 October — Brazil — president, Chamber of Deputies, Senate — today
- 9–10 October — Czech Republic — Senate, first round, one third of 81 seats
- 25 October — Serbia — parliament; Bulgaria — president
- 27 October — Israel — Knesset, 120 seats

### The ballot

Brazil, longest, because it has three offices and a rule people will mishear. Majority, not a plurality. Chamber, all of it, four years. Senate, two thirds of it, eight years, no second round. Governors vote today too, under the same presidential rule, and they are a sentence, not a fourth national office.

### How this government works

Bosnia is the structure lesson. There is no single head of state on the ballot. The chooser depends on which part of the country the voter lives in, and on which of the three seats that voter is allowed to fill. Explain the rule. Do not editorialize it into a score. Latvia is the comparison: one chamber, and a president chosen later by that chamber.

### The other chair

Latvia. One hundred seats. Parliamentary. Four years. No president on Saturday’s paper.

### Third chair

The Czech Senate next weekend. One third. Six-year terms. The government is not at stake, because the government comes from the Chamber of Deputies. A possible second round on 16 and 17 October stays conditional.

### The month ahead

Israel on 27 October, all 120 seats, a prime minister who is not directly elected. The United States on 3 November, and the presidency is not on that ballot either, for a different reason.

### What we will not say

- We will not name a Latvian prime minister, a Bosnian presidency, or a Brazilian president.
- We will not announce a Brazilian runoff as if it were already required.
- We will not say the Brazilian Senate has a second round. It does not.
- We will not score Bosnia’s electoral rule. We will describe it.

### Watch next

Sunday 11 October. The Czech Senate.

## Weekend of 10 October

- **Air:** Sunday 11 October, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Czech Senate, first round, Friday and Saturday

### Cold open

The Czech Republic has just elected one third of the Senate, on Friday and Saturday. Eighty-one seats in all, six-year terms, a third at a time. The Chamber of Deputies was not on the ballot, and the government comes from that chamber. A district that needs a second round would vote next weekend, 16 and 17 October. We do not know yet, on this script, whether any district does. We will not name a senator.

### On the desk

- 9–10 October — Czech Republic — Senate, first round
- 16–17 October — Czech Senate, second round — conditional
- 25 October — Bulgaria — president; Serbia — parliament
- 25 October — Brazil — presidential runoff — conditional
- 27 October — Israel — Knesset

### The ballot

Explain a two-round Senate district without pretending we already know the districts. First round, a majority wins the seat. If not, the top two return. The Senate cannot dismiss the government. Say that, because viewers will import Slovakia’s or Hungary’s parliament onto this vote.

### How this government works

A staggered senate is a brake. Two thirds of Czech senators were not facing the voters this weekend. The same idea, different numbers, is the United States Senate on 3 November: 35 seats up, 65 staying. Preview that in five minutes. Do not steal Israel’s week to do it.

### The other chair

What last Sunday did not finish. If Brazil’s first round is still uncalled on our desk, say “uncalled,” not a guess. If a commission majority is already published and a runoff is required, say that the 25th is now a real date. If a commission majority is published and someone won, say the runoff is off, and still do not dwell on the name beyond the commission’s sentence. This plan’s default line, because the file has no result, is: “We do not have a commission result. The 25th stays conditional.”

### What we will not say

- We will not say the Czech government fell.
- We will not invent which districts have a runoff.
- We will not declare Brazil.

### Watch next

Sunday 18 October, the conditional Czech show, and the preview of the 25th.

## Weekend of 17 October

- **Air:** Sunday 18 October, 120 minutes
- **Status:** conditional
- **Main election:** Czech Senate second round, only if required. Otherwise the preview of 25 October.

### Cold open A — a second round happened

Some Czech Senate districts did not finish last weekend. Those districts voted yesterday and the day before. The Chamber of Deputies still was not on the ballot. We will not name the senators. The rest of this show belongs to next Sunday: Serbia’s parliament, Bulgaria’s president, and Brazil’s presidential runoff only if the first round required it.

### Cold open B — no second round

The Czech Senate’s first round finished the seats that were up. There is no second-round show to do. A finished first round means the majority rule was met in the districts, not that we are going to read the names. Today we look at 25 October. Three lines may share that day, and only two of them are certain.

### On the desk

- 16–17 October — Czech Senate — second round — conditional
- 25 October — Serbia — parliament — confirmed as a snap election
- 25 October — Bulgaria — president — confirmed on the calendar
- 25 October — Brazil — president, runoff — conditional
- 27 October — Israel — Knesset

### The ballot

Whichever open you used, give the Czech facts five minutes and then leave them. The ballot block is 25 October.

Serbia: the National Assembly, called early. The president of Serbia is not a candidate. Parliamentary.

Bulgaria: the president, direct. Parliament was 19 April. Read the week’s rule card for a majority and for any runoff date. Do not invent a runoff.

Brazil: two scripts inside the block. If the runoff is required, the office is the presidency only. The Chamber and the Senate stay as they were elected on 4 October. If it is not required, say that in one sentence and spend the minutes on Serbia and Bulgaria.

### How this government works

Three ways an executive is chosen, if all three are alive: Serbia’s executive comes from parliament. Bulgaria’s president is elected by the public and is not the parliamentary prime minister. Brazil’s president is head of state and head of government, finished by a majority. Pick the ones that are actually voting. Do not teach a runoff that has been cancelled.

### The other chair

Israel on 27 October. All 120 seats. A coalition needs 61. The prime minister is not directly elected. The threshold is 3.25 percent. The Knesset that just ended served a full term, dissolved on 17 July 2026. Preview, five to eight minutes. The desk is the last Sunday of the month.

### Third chair

The United States, one screen only. Tuesday 3 November. House, 435. Senate, 35. Governors, 36 states. The presidency is not on the ballot. Tell the audience the full hour is 1 November.

### The month ahead

Five weekends, this is the fourth’s preview. After the 25th, Israel, then the midterm.

### What we will not say

## Weekend of 31 October

- **Air:** Sunday 1 November, 120 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** United States, Tuesday 3 November. Israel voted on Tuesday 27 October.

### Cold open

On Tuesday the United States elects every seat in the House of Representatives, 435 of them, and 35 seats in the Senate, and governors in 36 states. It does not elect a president. The new Congress takes office on 3 January 2027. Israel elected all 120 members of the Knesset on Tuesday. A government there needs 61. We have no winner for either country.

### On the desk

- 27 October — Israel — Knesset — held Tuesday
- 3 November — United States — House, Senate, 36 governors
- 7 November — New Zealand — House of Representatives
- 15 November — Cape Verde — president

### The ballot

The United States. Use the page in `show/elections/united-states.md` and do not shorten the “not the president” sentence.

House: all of it, two-year terms. Senate: the Class II seats from 2020, plus a special in Florida and a special in Ohio. Sixty-five senators are not on the ballot. A party can win most of the seats that are up and still not hold the chamber. Governors: state officers. They do not sit in Congress.

There is no national runoff. A plurality finishes the race. That is the difference from Brazil a month ago.

### How this government works

Separation, not parliamentarism. A new House majority can block a president. It cannot replace the president. There is no prime minister to defeat.

### The other chair

Israel. One hundred twenty seats, 61, no direct prime minister, 3.25 percent, a Knesset that had completed a full term. No coalition named by us.

### Third chair

New Zealand on Saturday. Parliamentary. Three-year term. The public does not elect a president. Five minutes. The full block is next Sunday, shared with the American desk.

### The month ahead

Cape Verde’s president on 15 November, the pair to its parliament on 17 May. Palestine’s parliament on 28 November.

### What we will not say

- We will not say the president is on the ballot.
- We will not call a governor a federal officer.
- We will not forget the word “special” for Florida and Ohio.
- We will not name a Knesset coalition or a chamber majority.
