# May 2026 — five weekends

Archive plans. No winners. India’s counting day is not a result.

## Weekend of 2 May

- **Air:** Sunday 3 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** India’s counting day is tomorrow, 4 May. Niue’s parliament was yesterday.

### Cold open

Tomorrow, 4 May, is the day the Election Commission of India scheduled the counting of five assemblies: Assam, Kerala, Puducherry, Tamil Nadu, and West Bengal. This show will explain what that day is. It will not read a result. The result is not in our file. Niue elected a parliament yesterday. That is one sentence. The Bahamas elect a House of Assembly on 12 May.

### On the desk

- 30 April — Antigua and Barbuda — House of Representatives — held Thursday, one sentence, no winner
- 2 May — Niue — parliament — yesterday
- 4 May — India — counting — result not recorded
- 12 May — Bahamas — House of Assembly
- 17 May — Cape Verde — parliament

### The ballot

There is no popular ballot today. Say so. Then explain counting: the votes that were cast in April are sorted. A counting day can finish an election administratively and still leave this desk without a winner, because we do not copy a television declaration. We wait for a commission source written into the file. That source is not there.

### How this government works

Remind the audience what those five counts are counts of. Five governments, not the Union government. West Bengal was two phases of one election. Puducherry is a Union territory.

### The other chair

Cape Verde’s parliament on 17 May, and the fact that the president of Cape Verde is not until 15 November. Start that pair. It pays off in November.

### What we will not say

- We will not name a chief minister.
- We will not say “the results are in” because counting is scheduled.
- We will not build an hour on Niue.

### Watch next

Sunday 10 May, the Bahamas as a preview, and a structure lesson on a small parliament.

## Weekend of 9 May

- **Air:** Sunday 10 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Bahamas, House of Assembly, Tuesday 12 May

### Cold open

Nothing votes today. On Tuesday the Bahamas elect a House of Assembly. The prime minister comes from that house. There is no presidential ballot beside it. India’s counting was last Monday. The result is still not recorded, and we are not going to repair that silence with a guess.

### On the desk

- 12 May — Bahamas — House of Assembly
- 17 May — Cape Verde — parliament
- 24 May — Cyprus — parliament
- 30 May — Malta — parliament, snap
- 31 May — Colombia — president, first round; Guinea — National Assembly

### The ballot

The Bahamas. Parliamentary. One chamber on the ballot, a government afterwards. Keep it short and exact.

### How this government works

Use the quiet Sunday for staggered chambers, because the audience has had a month of assemblies. A house that is fully renewed, like the Bahamas, is a different object from a senate that is renewed in thirds. The Czech Senate in October and the United States Senate in November are that second object. Plant them. Do not teach them fully tonight.

### The other chair

Colombia’s presidential first round on 31 May, with congress already elected on 8 March. One minute of reminder. The two-hour show is the last Sunday of this month.

### What we will not say

- We will not update India with an unsourced winner.
- We will not call the Bahamas a general election if only the house is on the ballot.

## Weekend of 23 May

- **Air:** Sunday 24 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Cyprus, parliament, this Sunday

### Cold open

Cyprus elects its House of Representatives today. The president is not on the ballot. Presidential and parliamentary elections in Cyprus do not share a year by design of this particular Sunday. Next Sunday is heavier: Malta’s snap parliamentary election, Colombia’s presidential first round, and Guinea’s National Assembly.

### On the desk

- 24 May — Cyprus — parliament — today
- 30 May — Malta — parliament
- 31 May — Colombia — president, first round
- 31 May — Guinea — National Assembly
- 7 June — Peru — presidential second round, as listed later; tonight say only that a runoff was possible

### The ballot

Cyprus. Chamber, not presidency. Term of the house is the thing being renewed. The government of the Republic is not replaced by a presidential count tonight because there is not one.

### How this government works

A presidential system and a parliamentary system can wear the same word, “parliament,” and mean a different power. In Cyprus the president is a strong office elected another day. In Malta, next Sunday, the parliament is where the prime minister comes from. Set those two sentences side by side.

### The other chair

The 31 May cluster, named. Colombia cannot elect a congress this Sunday; it already did. Guinea’s assembly date is a postponement onto 31 May. Malta is a snap election.

### What we will not say

- We will not elect a president of Cyprus tonight.
- We will not assume Colombia’s runoff.

## Weekend of 30 May

- **Air:** Sunday 31 May, 120 minutes
- **Status:** archive plan
- **Main election:** Colombia, president, first round; Guinea, National Assembly. Malta was Saturday.

### Cold open

Malta held a snap parliamentary election yesterday. The prime minister comes from that house. Today Colombia votes for a president, first round. Congress was elected on 8 March and is not on this ballot. A majority is required. If nobody has it, the second round is 21 June. Also today, Guinea votes for a National Assembly, on a date the election was postponed to. No winners.

### On the desk

- 30 May — Malta — parliament — yesterday
- 31 May — Colombia — president, first round — today
- 31 May — Guinea — National Assembly — today
- 1 June — Ethiopia — House of Peoples’ Representatives
- 7 June — Armenia, Jersey, Kosovo — parliaments; Peru — presidential second round

### The ballot

Colombia. Repeat the split until it is dull. March was the legislature. Today is the presidency. June may be the finish of the presidency. None of those days reopens congress.

### How this government works

A first-round majority is a finishing rule, not a style of campaign. Brazil will use the same idea in October. Colombia is the rehearsal. The host does not need Brazil tonight beyond one sentence.

### The other chair

Malta. Snap election. The term was a maximum. No prime minister named.

### Third chair

Guinea. Say the postponement in one sentence so the audience does not think a vote was held on an earlier day and again today. Then Ethiopia tomorrow, one sentence, carried properly next Sunday.

### The month ahead

June’s middle is a Swiss referendum Sunday. June’s end is Colombia’s second round, if this first round requires it, and New Caledonia’s parliament.

### What we will not say

- We will not name a Colombian president.
- We will not say congress was elected today.
- We will not name a Maltese prime minister.
- We will not invent an earlier Guinean election as if it had produced a chamber.
