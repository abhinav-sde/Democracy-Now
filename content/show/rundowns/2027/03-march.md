# March 2027 — four weekends

On the board. Indian assembly dates stay unannounced unless a notification exists by air time. No winners.

## Weekend of 6 March

- **Air:** Sunday 7 March, 120 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Estonia, today. Nigeria’s states were yesterday.

### Cold open

Yesterday Nigeria elected governors and state Houses of Assembly. That was the second Saturday of the general election, not a repeat of the presidential vote. Today Estonia elects the Riigikogu. One hundred and one seats. Four years. A party needs 5 percent. The prime minister comes from that chamber. The president of Estonia is not on the ballot. Polls are open until 20:00, and a paper ballot can still overwrite an electronic vote until then. We will not name a winner in either country.

### On the desk

- 2 March — Micronesia — parliament — one sentence, held Tuesday
- 6 March — Nigeria — governors and state assemblies — yesterday
- 7 March — Estonia — parliament — today
- March — Somaliland — parliament — no day
- 10 April — The Gambia — parliament
- 18 April — France — president, first round; Finland — parliament

### The ballot

Estonia, because it is today and because the mechanism is clean. Proportional parliament. Confidence of the chamber. A president chosen last September by the Riigikogu or a college, not by this queue of voters. Electronic voting all week, paper as the final word if the voter wants it.

### How this government works

Nigeria as the comparison of calendars, not of virtue. Two Saturdays, federal then state. Estonia, one Sunday, one office. A viewer who wants every African or European election to look like one template is the person this show is for.

### The other chair

The Gambia on 10 April. Parliament, after a president on 5 December 2026. Five-year presidential term already running. The assembly is the open office.

### Third chair

France and Finland, a month out, sharing 18 April and not sharing a system. Name the pair. The teaching starts on 21 March and 28 March.

### The month ahead

India. If the Election Commission has notified assembly dates, they go on this desk in one sentence and the 14 March show becomes a preview. If not, say “still no notification.”

### What we will not say

- We will not give Nigeria’s states a winner.
- We will not call today an Estonian presidential election.
- We will not invent a Somaliland Saturday.
- We will not invent an Indian phase.

### Watch next

Sunday 14 March. The Nigerian desk, if anything official can be read without becoming a result show, and the Indian test.

## Weekend of 13 March

### Cold open

The two Nigerian Saturdays are over. We still do not have winners in the file this script was written from. If the commission has published a final national sentence by today, the correspondent may read that sentence and sit down. The show is not a result program. The Gambia’s parliament is 10 April. France’s first round is 18 April.

- 10 April — The Gambia — parliament
- 17 April — France — overseas first round
- 18 April — France — first round; Finland — parliament
- India — undated unless a notification is in the studio

### The ballot

If an Indian notification is on the desk, this block becomes the assemblies: which states, which phases, which counting day, read from the notification only. If it is not, the block is the five-year assembly. Manipur, Goa, Punjab, and Uttarakhand are at the end of the term window reporting has described. Uttar Pradesh’s term runs to 22 May. None of those expiries is a poll.

### How this government works

A chief minister comes from an assembly. Notifying an election is an act of the Commission, not of this program. The discipline is the lesson.

### The other chair

The Gambia. Draw December 2026 and April 2027 on one calendar. President, then parliament. The split wider than Cape Verde’s.

### What we will not say

## Weekend of 20 March

- **Air:** Sunday 21 March, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. Structure: France against Finland.

### Cold open

Four weeks from today, France and Finland vote on the same Sunday. France elects a president. Finland elects a parliament. They are not having the same election. France’s second round, if nobody wins a majority, is 2 May. Finland does not have that second round. The French National Assembly is not on either day.

### On the desk

- 10 April — The Gambia — parliament
- 17 and 18 April — France — president, first round
- 18 April — Finland — Eduskunta, 200 seats
- 2 May — France — second round — conditional
- April, no day — Andorra — parliament

### The ballot

No ballot today. The block is the pair, taught early.

France. Semi-presidential. The public elects the president. The president names a prime minister who has to survive in the National Assembly. Overseas France votes the Saturday before. A first-round majority cancels 2 May.

Finland. Parliamentary. Two hundred seats. Four years. Proportional. The prime minister comes from the Eduskunta. The president of Finland is on another cycle and is not a candidate.

### How this government works

One Sunday, two choosers of two different executives. France’s public is choosing a president. Finland’s public is choosing a chamber. Say it until it is the only sentence a viewer remembers.

### The other chair

Andorra’s parliament, sometime in April, no day. One sentence. Do not give it a Sunday.

### What we will not say

- We will not announce French legislative elections. We do not have a day.
- We will not treat 2 May as certain.
- We will not put the Finnish president on the ballot.

### Watch next

Sunday 28 March, the overseas-Saturday detail. Sunday 4 April, the last preview, with The Gambia.

## Weekend of 27 March

- **Air:** Sunday 28 March, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. France, the calendar inside the calendar.

### Cold open

France does not vote on one day even in the first round. Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon, Saint-Barthélemy, Saint-Martin, and French Polynesia vote on Saturday 17 April. Metropolitan France votes on Sunday 18 April. The same split, if a second round is required, is Saturday 1 May and Sunday 2 May. Finland votes once, on the 18th.

### On the desk

- 10 April — The Gambia — parliament
- 17 April — France overseas — president, first round
- 18 April — France — first round; Finland — parliament
- 2 May — France — conditional
- 3 May — Burundi — president

### The ballot

The time-zone Saturday. It is part of the same round, not a different election and not an early result the Sunday show may treat as the whole country. Say that plainly, because the graphics will want to.

### How this government works

Repeat semi-presidential in four sentences, no more. Public elects president. President appoints prime minister. Assembly can bring the government down. Assembly is not being elected in April. Then stop.

### The other chair

Burundi on 3 May, a president, the day after the conditional French second round. The May shows will have two opens. Warn the audience now.

### What we will not say

- We will not call Saturday’s overseas vote a completed French election.
- We will not invent a legislative date.
- We will not start naming French candidates. This book does not.

### Watch next

Sunday 4 April, 120 minutes. Gambia’s last preview, and France’s.
