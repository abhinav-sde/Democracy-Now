# May 2027 — five weekends

On the board. France is conditional. Guatemala stays preliminary. No winners.

## Weekend of 1 May

- **Air:** Sunday 2 May, 120 minutes
- **Status:** conditional
- **Main election:** France, only under Open A. Burundi is Monday.

### Cold open A — the second round is on

Nobody won a majority in France on 18 April. Overseas territories voted yesterday. Metropolitan France votes today, for a president, between the top two. The National Assembly is not on the ballot. Burundi elects a president tomorrow. We will not name either president.

### Cold open B — the second round is not on

France finished its presidential election on 18 April, because someone won a majority. There is no vote today. We will not re-litigate it, and we will not make this show a coronation. Tomorrow Burundi elects a president. That is the ballot we actually have.

### On the desk

- 1 May — France overseas — only under Open A
- 2 May — France — only under Open A
- 3 May — Burundi — president — Monday
- 6 June — Mexico — Chamber of Deputies, 500 seats
- 27 June — Guatemala — general election — preliminary

### The ballot

Under Open A: the presidency, the top-two rule, the overseas Saturday as the same round. Under Open B: ten minutes on what a finished first round means, then the entire ballot block is Burundi.

Burundi. President. Confirmed day. No parliamentary line added by us.

### How this government works

Semi-presidential, under either open. The president who emerges still appoints a prime minister who has to live with an assembly elected on a different day, a day we do not have. That is the sentence that keeps this from being a “France has decided everything” show.

### The other chair

Mexico on 6 June. Five hundred deputies. Three hundred from districts, two hundred from lists. Not the president. Not the Senate. A midterm of one chamber. Preview. The full hour is 6 June.

### Third chair

Guatemala, 27 June, preliminary. President, congress, municipalities, Central American Parliament deputies. Second round 22 August only if needed and only if the convocation decree confirms it. Say preliminary every time.

### The month ahead

Five Sundays. After today: Burundi’s desk, then Mexico’s mechanism, then Guatemala’s preliminary calendar, then Mexico’s last preview.

### What we will not say

- We will not run Open A if the first round produced a majority.
- We will not name a French or Burundian president.
- We will not call Mexico’s June vote a presidential election.
- We will not call Guatemala’s dates final.

### Watch next

Sunday 9 May. Burundi’s desk, and Mexico.

## Weekend of 8 May

### Cold open

Burundi elected a president on Monday. We will say the office and the day. We will not say the winner unless we are reading a commission sentence that arrived after this script, and even then the show returns to the office. France is not today’s subject, except one sentence on whether 2 May happened. Mexico renews the Chamber of Deputies on 6 June.

### On the desk

- 3 May — Burundi — president — held
- 6 June — Mexico — Chamber of Deputies
- 27 June — Guatemala — preliminary
- June, no day — Mongolia — president

### The ballot

Burundi, short. Then Mexico, because June is the next mechanism the audience must not mishear.

### How this government works

A midterm chamber. The Mexican president sits for six years and is not a candidate. Senators sit for six years and are not up. All 500 deputies are up. Local elections share the Sunday — governors, state congresses, municipalities, more than 19,000 posts in the institute’s own description — and they are one sentence, not the federal story.

### The other chair

Mongolia’s president, sometime in June, no day. Do not invent a second-round Sunday.

### What we will not say

- We will not declare Burundi.
- We will not schedule a Mongolian runoff.

### Watch next

Sunday 16 May. Mexico’s chamber, taught properly.

## Weekend of 15 May

- **Air:** Sunday 16 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. Mexico’s chamber.

### Cold open

Nothing votes today. On 6 June Mexico elects 500 deputies and does not elect a president. This hour is that sentence, with the numbers attached, so election Sunday can be about the rule rather than the orientation.

### On the desk

- 6 June — Mexico — 300 district seats, 200 list seats
- 27 June — Guatemala — preliminary first round
- 22 August — Guatemala — presidential second round — preliminary and conditional

### The ballot

Empty. Then the chamber. How a mixed member system puts two kinds of deputy in one house. What the house can do to a president who still has years left: pass laws, or refuse them. What it cannot do: become the presidency.

### How this government works

Compare with the United States last November. There, the whole House and a third of the Senate, and no president. Here, the whole chamber of deputies, no Senate, no president. Both are midterms. They are not copies.

### The other chair

Guatemala’s preliminary character. The TSE showed parties a calendar. The convocation decree is the thing that turns preliminary into confirmed. Until you are holding that decree, you say preliminary.

### What we will not say

- We will not predict the Mexican chamber.
- We will not firm Guatemala up by enthusiasm.

### Watch next

Sunday 23 May, Guatemala’s offices. Sunday 30 May, Mexico’s last preview.

## Weekend of 22 May

- **Air:** Sunday 23 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. Guatemala, preliminary, explained.

### Cold open

Mexico is two weeks away and we will not steal this Sunday from a date that is easy to over-believe. Guatemala’s first round is aimed at 27 June. A presidential second round is aimed at 22 August, and only if nobody wins an absolute majority. Both dates are preliminary. The ballot, when it is real, is a president and vice president, 160 deputies, 20 deputies to the Central American Parliament, and municipal corporations.

### On the desk

- 6 June — Mexico — Chamber of Deputies
- 22 January was the aimed convocation — if it happened, say so from the decree; if not, say the calendar is still preliminary
- 27 June — Guatemala — preliminary
- 22 August — Guatemala — preliminary and conditional

### The ballot

List the offices from the TSE note. Absolute majority for the presidency. Congress does not use that same second round. Say so, so a viewer does not think every Guatemalan office is unfinished until August.

### How this government works

An absolute majority is half plus something the statute defines. Read the statute’s sentence from the card. Do not round it into a feeling.

### The other chair

Mexico, five minutes, only to keep 6 June warm. The full preview is next Sunday.

### What we will not say

- We will not call 27 June confirmed without the decree.
- We will not put the Guatemalan president’s second round on the calendar as a fact.

### Watch next

Sunday 30 May. Mexico. Then Sunday 6 June, election day.

## Weekend of 29 May

- **Air:** Sunday 30 May, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Mexico, next Sunday

### Cold open

Next Sunday Mexico elects the Chamber of Deputies. Five hundred seats. The presidency is not on the ballot. The Senate is not on the ballot. Local offices share the day and are not the federal lead. This is a midterm of one house. We will not predict a majority.

### On the desk

- 6 June — Mexico — Chamber of Deputies
- 27 June — Guatemala — preliminary
- June — Mongolia — president — no day

### The ballot

The last pass. 300 and 200. Six-year president, staying. Six-year Senate, staying. What a chamber election can change: the agenda of legislation. What it cannot change: the person in the presidency.

### How this government works

Use the United States comparison once more, shorter than on 16 May. Then stop teaching and tell the audience what next Sunday’s lower third will say: “Mexico, Chamber of Deputies, not the presidency.”

### The other chair

Guatemala, still preliminary, one minute. Mongolia, no day, one minute.

### What we will not say

- We will not call it a general election.
- We will not name a favorite.

### Watch next

Sunday 6 June, 120 minutes.
