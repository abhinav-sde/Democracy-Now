# December 2027 — four weekends

On the board. No dated national election. The year show does not become a results reel.

## Weekend of 4 December

- **Air:** Sunday 5 December, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Italy, only if a decree set this week

### Cold open

Italy’s next election of the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate, if the parliament is not dissolved earlier, falls inside a legal window that ends this month. A window is not a polling day. If a decree is in the studio, this script is replaced by the decree and we still do not name a winner. If there is no decree, there is no Italian election to cover, and the hour is about what those two chambers are.

### On the desk

- Italy — Chamber and Senate — no day, unless the decree drawer says otherwise
- Fiji — parliament — last check is next week
- No other dated national line

### The ballot

Default: no ballot. Then the chambers. Italy elects both houses of a parliament. The government needs the confidence of parliament. A referendum, like the one on 22 March 2026, does not elect these chambers. That distinction is a year old and still useful.

### How this government works

Dissolution, one more time, because this is the last large parliamentary system whose lid we are watching. The people who can end a parliament early are not this show. When they do it, they create a date. They have not handed us one.

### The other chair

Fiji. Carried as undated since the 2026 calendar. Next Sunday we say, plainly, whether a day ever appeared. Tonight, only that the check is coming.

### What we will not say

- We will not set Italy for a Sunday in December.
- We will not recap 2026’s Italian referendum as if it had been a general election.

### Watch next

Sunday 12 December. The Fiji check, and any decree that arrived.

## Weekend of 18 December

- **Air:** Sunday 19 December, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. The year, as designs.

### Cold open

There is no election on this weekend. We are not going to invent one to end the year. We are going to name six designs that 2027 actually put on the calendar. We are not going to name the people who won them. The file this show works from does not have those names.

### On the desk

No new dates. The six designs.

### The ballot

Say them in order:

1. On 30 January, Germany’s president was chosen by a Federal Convention. The public did not vote. The same week, Kyrgyzstan’s president was chosen by the public.
2. Nigeria used two Saturdays. A president and a National Assembly on 20 February. Governors and state assemblies on 6 March. The commission’s dates, not 16 January.
3. On 7 March, Estonia elected a parliament. Its president was not on the ballot.
4. On 18 April, France voted for a president and Finland voted for a parliament. The French second round on 2 May existed only if nobody won a majority.
5. On 6 June, Mexico renewed the whole Chamber of Deputies and did not renew the president or the Senate.
6. On 24 October, Argentina’s public elected a president. Switzerland’s public, the same day, elected two chambers and did not elect the executive.

If a conditional show — France in May, Guatemala in August — took Open B, say that in the sentence. Do not narrate a second round that was not held.

### How this government works

The sentence under all six. The interesting fact in an election is who the chooser is, and what they are allowed to finish. A date without that fact is a headline. A date with it is this show.

### The other chair

What remains open on purpose: India’s assemblies if they were never notified, Spain’s and Poland’s and Italy’s lids if they were never turned into days, Libya’s goal, Fiji if last Sunday was still “no day.” Open is not failure.

### What we will not say

- We will not run a montage of winners.
- We will not grade the year.
- We will not score a country.

### Watch next

Sunday 26 December. Ninety minutes. The desk is short. The structure lessons are tape.

## Weekend of 25 December

- **Air:** Sunday 26 December, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none

### Cold open

It is the weekend of Christmas, and the calendar is still empty. We are not shortening the show below an hour and a half, and we are not filling it with news we do not have. Most of this hour is tape: the six designs from last Sunday, told once each, by the structure editor, without a guest predicting anything. The live desk is the open, a two-minute check that no decree arrived over the holiday, and the close.

### On the desk

- Any decree dated 23–26 December — if none, say none
- The paper calendar for the first Sundays of 2028 is not this book’s job tonight

### The ballot

The live check. If a decree arrived, you do not read the taped hour as if it were ignorant of the decree. You say the day, you say the office, you say we will not name a winner, and you cut one tape to make room. If nothing arrived, you say “nothing arrived” and you play the tapes.

### How this government works

The tapes, in the same order as last Sunday. Germany and Kyrgyzstan. Nigeria’s two Saturdays. Estonia. France and Finland. Mexico. Argentina and Switzerland. Each tape ends on the chooser and the office. None ends on a name.

### The other chair

The close, live. A weekly show continues because the next parliament somewhere will be dissolved, or a commission will finally print a day, or a second round will become real. The desk will be here. It will still not invent the winner.

### What we will not say

- We will not wish a result into the year.
- We will not call a tape a live count.
- We will not drop below ninety minutes by pretending the holiday deleted the job.

### Watch next

The next book starts when the next commission prints a day. This book stops.
