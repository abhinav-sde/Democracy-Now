# January 2027 — five weekends

On the board. Germany’s Convention is confirmed. Nigeria’s January date is a mistake we will say out loud.

## Weekend of 2 January

- **Air:** Sunday 3 January, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none today. The false Nigeria date is the reason to air.

### Cold open

A widely copied calendar still says Nigeria elects a president, a Senate, and a House on 16 January. The Independent National Electoral Commission said otherwise on 13 February 2026. Presidential election and National Assembly: Saturday 20 February. Governors and state assemblies: Saturday 6 March. This show follows the commission. The 16th will still get a Sunday, and that Sunday’s job is to repeat this correction, not to cover a vote.

### On the desk

- 16 January — Nigeria — ignore as election day
- 27 January — Kyrgyzstan — president — confirmed
- 30 January — Germany — federal president — indirect, Federal Convention
- 20 February — Nigeria — president, Senate, House
- 6 March — Nigeria — governors and state assemblies

### The ballot

Empty today. Say it. Then the two January votes that are real: a popular presidential election in Kyrgyzstan on the 27th, and an indirect presidential election in Germany on the 30th.

### How this government works

Same office-name, different chooser. Kyrgyzstan’s public elects a president. Germany’s public does not. The Federal Convention does: every member of the Bundestag, plus an equal number chosen by the state parliaments. The chancellor is not on that ballot. The president is head of state.

### The other chair

India’s assemblies that are reaching the end of their terms — Manipur, Goa, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh — have no Election Commission schedule in this book. Do not give them a January date. Mention the gap so a guest does not invent one.

### What we will not say

- We will not put Nigeria on the 16 January lower third as a live election.
- We will not invent Indian phase dates.

### Watch next

Sunday 10 January, a structure hour on Nigeria’s two Saturdays. Sunday 17 January, the correction, on the day the stale calendar named.

### Cold open

Nothing is on the ballot today. In six weeks Nigeria votes for a president and a National Assembly. Two weeks after that, it votes for governors and state assemblies. The gap is the design. We are teaching it now so February is not the first time the audience hears it.

### On the desk

- 27 January — Kyrgyzstan — president
- 30 January — Germany — Federal Convention
- 20 February — Nigeria — federal offices
- 6 March — Nigeria — state offices

### The ballot

Empty. Then the Nigerian rule, read slowly. The president needs the highest number of votes and at least a quarter of the votes in two thirds of the states and the Federal Capital Territory. If not, a runoff. Do not predict the runoff. The Senate and the House are the same Saturday as the president. The states are not.

### How this government works

A federation can refuse to put every office on one day. The United States midterm refused to include the president. Nigeria’s general election refuses to include the governors on the presidential Saturday. Both are choices about what a single day is allowed to change.
