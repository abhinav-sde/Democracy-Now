# October 2027 — five weekends

On the board. One confirmed Sunday does the work of the month. No winners.

## Weekend of 2 October

- **Air:** Sunday 3 October, 90 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** none. Argentina and Switzerland, three weeks out.

### Cold open

On 24 October Argentina elects a president, a class of senators, and the Chamber of Deputies. The same Sunday, Switzerland elects the National Council and the Council of States. Switzerland does not elect the Federal Council. That body is chosen by parliament in December. Argentina does elect a president, and the rule that finishes the presidency is whatever the statute in force says. If that text is on this desk, we will read the operative sentence. If it is not, we will not replace it with the rule from the last election.

### On the desk

- 24 October — Argentina — the offices
- 24 October — Switzerland — two chambers
- October — Oman — parliament — still no day, unless a day has been set; then say it

### The ballot

Read the Argentine sentence, or read the refusal. Then the offices, so the audience knows what the sentence applies to. A presidential threshold does not finish a senate class. Say that.

### How this government works

Switzerland’s December, explained in October so nobody waits for a popular vote that is not coming. Seven seats. Parliament votes. One member at a time. The October election changes who does the choosing. It is not itself the choice.

### The other chair

Oman, updated or still undated.

### What we will not say

- We will not predict a runoff in Argentina.
- We will not say the Swiss government is on the October ballot.

### Watch next

Sunday 10 October. The same pair, from the voter’s side.

## Weekend of 23 October

- **Air:** Sunday 24 October, 120 minutes
- **Status:** on the board
- **Main election:** Argentina and Switzerland, today

### Cold open

Two countries vote today, and only one of them is electing a president. Argentina’s ballot has the presidency, the Chamber of Deputies, and the Senate seats that are up. Switzerland’s ballot has the National Council, 200 seats, and the Council of States, 46 seats, under cantonal rules. The Swiss executive is elected later, by the parliament these voters are choosing. We will not name a president, a deputy, or a councillor.

### On the desk

- 24 October — Argentina — today
- 24 October — Switzerland — today
- November — Marshall Islands — parliament — undated
- Poland — if a decree has appeared, the day; if not, still a deadline

### The ballot

Argentina first, the offices, the statute’s finishing rule read from the page. Then Switzerland, the two houses, and the sentence about December.

### How this government works

The lesson of the whole year, on the night it is easiest to see. “President” is an office. It is on one of these ballots. It is not on the other. The countries are voting on the same Sunday because the calendar fell that way, not because their constitutions agree.

### The other chair

December’s Federal Council, five minutes, so the audience knows what to watch for when this parliament sits. Not a popular vote. Not tonight.

### Third chair

The Marshall Islands, undated, one minute. Poland, only if you are correcting a graphic that treats early November as election day.

### The month ahead

November’s Sundays are the undated remainder: Marshall Islands, the small parliaments, Libya and Cameroon as goals rather than days, and Poland’s lid.

### What we will not say

- We will not crown a Swiss Federal Councillor. That vote is not today.
