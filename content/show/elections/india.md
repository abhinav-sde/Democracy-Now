# India — what the weekend show explains

India is a parliamentary union. The public elects the Lok Sabha. The prime minister is the person who can command that house. The president is head of state and is not popularly elected. State assemblies choose state governments. A state election does not change the Union government.

The show never reads a winner that the Election Commission has not published into the desk. For April 2026, the result is not recorded.

Structure, said once and then assumed:

- Each assembly sits for five years unless it is dissolved.
- The chief minister is chosen from the assembly, the way the prime minister is chosen from the Lok Sabha.
- These five elections do not elect a single “Indian government.” They elect five governments.
- West Bengal’s two phases are one election. Do not treat 23 April and 29 April as two results.

## Rajya Sabha, 2026 — indirect

The Council of States is not elected by the public. State assemblies elect members, staggered, six-year terms, one third at a time.

The calendar’s windows:

- 16 March 2026 — 37 seats across 10 states.
- 18 June 2026 — a further round.
- November 2026 — a further window, day not set in this book.

The March and June Sundays mention it in “On the desk” as indirect. It does not lead a show that also has a popular vote, except the quiet lesson “some chambers are elected by other politicians.”

## President, July 2027 — indirect, undated

The President is elected by an electoral college: elected members of both houses of Parliament, and elected members of the state legislative assemblies. Votes are weighted so that Parliament and the assemblies balance, and so that states are weighted by population. Nominated members do not vote.

The 2027 calendar says July and does not give a day. The July 2027 shows keep the slot.

- Until a day is notified, the show explains the college.
- When a day is notified, the nearest Sunday becomes the ballot show.
- There is no popular vote to cover, and no exit poll to refuse, because the electors are a few thousand office-holders. The host says that.

The term is five years. The president does not run the government. That sentence is the point of the July lesson, next to Germany’s Federal Convention on 30 January 2027, which is the same kind of act in another country.
