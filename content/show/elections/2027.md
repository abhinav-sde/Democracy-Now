# Elections of 2027 — structure for the show

Dates below come from the [2027 national electoral calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_national_electoral_calendar) unless a commission notice is named. Where they disagree, the show follows the commission.

Indian state assemblies due in 2027 have **no Election Commission schedule** in this book. They are in [india.md](india.md). They are not given a fake polling day here.

No winner is named for any line.

---

## Dated

### 20 February and 6 March — Nigeria, general election
**Use these days, not 16 January.**

On 13 February 2026 the Independent National Electoral Commission published the notice: presidential election and National Assembly on **20 February 2027**; governorships and state Houses of Assembly on **6 March 2027**.

[INEC, 13 February 2026](https://www.inecnigeria.org/press-conference-by-the-inec-chairman-professor-joash-o-amupitan-san-on-friday-13th-february-2026/)

The Wikipedia calendar still lists 16 January for president, Senate, and House. That line is stale for this show. The 16 January weekend exists to say so, not to cover an election.

Structure:

- **20 February, Saturday.** President, Senate, and House of Representatives. The president is head of state and head of government. A winner needs the highest number of votes and at least one quarter of the votes in two thirds of the states and the Federal Capital Territory. If nobody does, there is a runoff. The show reads that rule. It does not predict a runoff.
- **6 March, Saturday.** 36 governors and the state assemblies. These are state governments. They are not the president and they are not the National Assembly.
- The lesson of the pair: Nigeria does not put every office on one Saturday.

Show: preview 13 February, national ballot 20 February, state ballot 6 March.

### 27 January — Kyrgyzstan, president
Direct presidential election. Confirmed on the calendar from a 2025 date-setting report. The parliament is not this line. The show does not invent the term length if the week’s law card is not in the studio; it says “president, 27 January, confirmed.”

Show: weekend of 23 January as preview, weekend of 30 January as the desk (the vote was Wednesday).

### 30 January — Germany, president, indirect
**Confirmed** by the President of the Bundestag. The 18th Federal Convention meets in the Bundestag on Saturday 30 January 2027 to elect the federal president.

[Deutscher Bundestag, 26 February 2026](https://www.bundestag.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/2026/pm-260226-bundesversammlung-1150358)

Structure:

- The public does not vote.
- The Federal Convention is every member of the Bundestag, plus an equal number of people chosen by the 16 state parliaments.
- The federal president is head of state, not head of government. The chancellor is chosen by the Bundestag and is not on this day’s ballot.
- The Convention has to meet at least 30 days before the president’s term ends. That term ends at the close of 18 March 2027, so the last legal day would have been 16 February. The Bundestag set 30 January, earlier than the deadline.
- Early rounds need an absolute majority. The show does not invent a winner if the first round fails; it explains that later rounds exist.

Show: weekend of 30 January. That Saturday is the Convention. Sunday’s show is the explanation, not a popular-vote night.

### 28 February — El Salvador, president and Legislative Assembly
**Confirmed** by the Supreme Electoral Tribunal for 28 February 2027. Both offices, one Sunday. A general election, not a midterm.

The show does not improvise the re-election rule. It reads the TSE notice in the studio. If the notice is not in the studio, the host says the offices and the day and stops.

Show: weekend of 27 February.

### 2 March — Micronesia, parliament
Congressional election in the Federated States of Micronesia. The president is chosen afterwards by Congress from among certain members, not by a separate popular presidential ballot. If the week’s law card is missing, say “parliament” only.

Show: weekend of 6 March, one block.

### 7 March — Estonia, parliament
**Confirmed by the Estonian election authority**, which the international calendar had left as “March” without a day.

[Valimised.ee, Riigikogu 2027](https://www.valimised.ee/en/riigikogu-elections-2027/election-activities-and-deadlines-election-riigikogu-2027)

- Election day is Sunday 7 March 2027. Voting is 9:00 to 20:00.
- Electronic voting runs 1–6 March and can be overwritten by a paper ballot until 20:00 on election day.
- Advance voting in designated places is 1–4 March; in all districts, 5–6 March.
- The Riigikogu has 101 seats, a four-year term, proportional representation, and a 5 percent threshold. The government needs the confidence of that chamber.
- The president is not on this ballot. Estonia’s president was an indirect election on 2 September 2026.

Show: weekend of 6 March. That Sunday is election day. Share the two-hour clock with Nigeria’s state elections the day before.

### March, no day — Somaliland, parliament
Listed for March with no day. **Undated inside the month.** The show on 6 March says “March, day not set” and does not pick one. Somaliland’s status is disputed; the show does not decide it.

### 10 April — The Gambia, parliament
**Confirmed** on the calendar from the Gambian election commission’s 2026–27 date announcement. The president was elected on 5 December 2026, for five years. The National Assembly is this later day. Split offices, split dates. That is the lesson, the pair to Zambia’s same-week general election in 2026.

Show: weekend of 10 April.

### 18 April and 2 May — France, president
**Use both days.** The Council of Ministers of 1 July 2026 set them.

[Service-Public, 2 July 2026](https://www.service-public.gouv.fr/particuliers/actualites/A15053?lang=en)

- First round: Sunday 18 April 2027.
- Second round: Sunday 2 May 2027, only if no candidate wins a majority on 18 April.
- Guadeloupe, Martinique, French Guiana, Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon, Saint-Barthélemy, Saint-Martin, and French Polynesia vote the day before: Saturday 17 April and, if needed, Saturday 1 May.

Structure:

- France is semi-presidential. The public elects the president. The president appoints a prime minister who must survive in the National Assembly.
- The National Assembly is **not** on these dates. No legislative day is in this book. Do not announce one.
- A first-round majority would cancel 2 May. The 1 May / 2 May show has two opens.

Show: preview 3 April and 10 April, first round 17 April, conditional second round 1 May.

### 18 April — Finland, parliament
**Confirmed.** The Eduskunta, 200 seats, four-year term, proportional. The prime minister comes from that chamber. The president of Finland is on a different cycle and is not this ballot. Finland and France share a Sunday and do not share a system: Finland is parliamentary, France is electing a president.

[Vaalit.fi schedule](https://vaalit.fi/en/schedule-in-the-parliamentary-elections), as cited by the 2027 calendar.

Show: weekend of 17 April, as the other chair beside France.

### April, no day — Andorra, parliament
**Undated inside April.** The 3 April show gives it one sentence and does not invent a Sunday.

### 3 May — Burundi, president
**Confirmed** on the calendar from the national election commission’s 2027 calendar release. Presidential. The show does not invent a parliamentary election on the same day; the line is the president.

Show: weekend of 1 May, beside the conditional French second round.

### 6 June — Mexico, Chamber of Deputies
**Confirmed** by the National Electoral Institute. Election day is Sunday 6 June 2027. The federal process renews the 500 seats of the Chamber of Deputies: 300 by single-member districts, 200 by proportional representation.

[INE, Proceso Electoral Federal 2026–2027](https://ine.mx/voto-y-elecciones/elecciones-2027/)

- The presidency is not on the ballot. The president’s term is six years.
- The Senate is not renewed on this day. Senators sit six years.
- This is a midterm of the chamber, the Mexican cousin of a United States midterm, with one difference the show should say: Mexico renews the whole chamber, and the president still has years left.
- The same Sunday, local bodies elect governors, state congresses, and municipal offices. INE has described more than 19,000 local posts in concurrence. They are not the federal story. One sentence.

Show: preview 29 May, ballot 5 June.

### 27 June and 22 August — Guatemala, president and Congress
**Preliminary**, not the final convocation decree.

The Supreme Electoral Tribunal’s preliminary calendar, presented to parties and still awaiting the convocation decree, sets:

- Convocation aimed at 22 January 2027.
- General election, first round: Sunday 27 June 2027. President and vice president, 160 deputies, 20 deputies to the Central American Parliament, and municipal corporations.
- Presidential second round: Sunday 22 August 2027, only if nobody wins an absolute majority, and only if the decree confirms the day.

[TSE preliminary calendar](https://tse.org.gt/comunicacion/noticias/tse-presenta-cronograma-electoral-preliminar-para-las-elecciones-generales-y-de-diputados-al-parlamento-centroamericano-2027)

The host says “preliminary” every time these dates are spoken, until a convocation decree replaces that word with “confirmed.”

Show: first round weekend of 26 June; conditional second round weekend of 21 August.

### June, no day — Mongolia, president
**Undated inside June.** Direct presidential systems in Mongolia have used a majority and a second round; the show does not announce a second-round day that nobody has set. The 12 June show says “June, day not set.”

### July, no day — India, president, indirect
The electoral college of Parliament and the state assemblies. Not a popular vote. No day in this book. See [india.md](india.md).

Show: the July Sundays hold the slot. If a day is announced, the nearest Sunday becomes the ballot show. Until then they are structure shows.

### 10 August — Kenya, president, National Assembly, and Senate
**Confirmed** on the calendar, and the electoral commission has published a timetable toward Tuesday 10 August 2027.

Reported pieces of that timetable, which the correspondent re-reads from the gazette before air:

- Public officers who want to stand resign by 10 February 2027, six months out.
- Campaigns end 7 August 2027, 48 hours before the vote.

[Kenya News Agency on the IEBC gazette](https://www.kenyanews.go.ke/iebc-targets-28-5-million-voters-in-next-polls/)

Structure:

- One general election: president, National Assembly, Senate.
- The president needs more than half the votes and at least a quarter of the votes in 24 of the 47 counties. If not, a runoff. Do not predict the runoff.
- Governors are county officers on the same general-election cycle. Mention them. Do not make them the lead.

Show: preview 7 August, desk 14 August (the vote is Tuesday).

### 24 August — Angola, parliament
**Confirmed** on the calendar for 24 August. The public elects the National Assembly. Under Angola’s system the head of the national list of the largest party becomes president. Say that only if the week’s law card is in the studio; otherwise say “parliament, 24 August” and do not invent a separate presidential ballot. The calendar’s line is the parliament.

Show: weekend of 21 August as watch-next, weekend of 28 August as the desk.

### 24 October — Argentina, president, Senate, and Chamber of Deputies
**Confirmed** as the day on the 2027 calendar. A general election: the presidency and both chambers (the Senate only in part; Argentine senators sit staggered terms).

The winning rule for the presidency has been amended before. **Do not recite the 2023 threshold from memory.** The 2 October show reads whatever statute is in force that week. If the statute is not in the studio, the host says the offices and the day and says the threshold will be read when the text is on the desk.

Show: previews 2, 9, and 16 October; ballot weekend of 23 October.

### 24 October — Switzerland, National Council and Council of States
**Confirmed.** The Federal Statistical and chancellery calendars put the federal election on this Sunday.

- National Council: 200 seats, proportional, four years. The whole house.
- Council of States: 46 seats, two from most cantons and one from the half-cantons. The method is cantonal, not one national rule.
- The Federal Council, the seven-member executive, is **not** on this ballot. Parliament elects it afterwards, in December, one member at a time.

That last fact is the lesson beside Argentina, where the public does elect the president on the same Sunday.

Show: weekend of 23 October, sharing the two-hour clock with Argentina.

### October, no day — Oman, parliament
**Undated inside October.** One sentence on 2 October. The Consultative Assembly’s election is not a presidential election.

### November, no day — Marshall Islands, parliament
**Undated inside November.** Parliamentary. The president is chosen by the Nitijela from among its members, not by a separate public vote. Say that if the law card is in the studio.

Show: weekend of 13 November.

## How the year teaches, if you only remember six Sundays

| Sunday | Lesson |
| --- | --- |
| 31 January | Germany’s president is elected by a convention. Kyrgyzstan’s president, that same week, is elected by the public. |
| 21 February | Nigeria puts the nation and the states on two different Saturdays. |
| 7 March | Estonia’s parliament is elected by the public. Its president is not. |
| 18 April | France elects a president. Finland, the same day, elects a parliament. |
| 6 June | Mexico renews the whole Chamber of Deputies and does not renew the president. |
| 24 October | Argentina’s public elects a president. Switzerland’s public, the same day, elects two chambers and does not elect the executive. |
