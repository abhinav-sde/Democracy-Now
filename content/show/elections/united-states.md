# United States — the 2026 midterm, for a two-hour weekend

Election day is **Tuesday 3 November 2026**. Confirmed.

The presidency is not on the ballot. The show’s job is to stop a viewer thinking that it is.

Sources already on the website desk: the Federal Election Commission’s election and voting pages, and the 2026 national electoral calendar. Governors are state officers on the same Tuesday; they are not members of the federal government.

## What is elected

| Office | What is on the ballot | Term | Takes office |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| House of Representatives | All 435 voting seats | 2 years | 3 January 2027, the 120th Congress |
| Senate | 35 seats | 6 years | 3 January 2027 |
| Governors | 36 states | State term, usually 4 years | By each state’s own date, not 3 January |

The 35 Senate seats are the 33 Class II seats last elected in 2020, plus special elections in Florida (the Rubio seat) and Ohio (the Vance seat).

There is no national second round. A plurality in the state, or in the district, finishes that race. That is the comparison with Brazil, which votes the month before and may need 25 October.

Territorial governors are not given their own rows.

## What is not elected

- The president. That term runs to January 2029.
- The other 65 senators. They stay.
- A prime minister. There is none. The president is both head of state and head of government, and the president is not a candidate this year.
- A government in the parliamentary sense. A change of majority in the House or the Senate changes what can pass, and what can be blocked. It does not of itself replace the president.

## How the two chambers differ

Say this slowly. It is the structure lesson.

- Every House seat is at risk every two years. The whole chamber can change character in one night.
- Only a third of the Senate is at risk on a normal cycle. In 2026 it is a third plus two specials. A party can win most of the seats that are up and still not hold the chamber, because 65 senators are not on the ballot.
- The two specials exist because those seats fell vacant mid-term. A special election finishes the remainder of a six-year term; it does not reset the class. The correspondent checks the remainder before air and does not guess it.

## Governors

Thirty-six states elect a governor on the same Tuesday. A governor runs a state. A governor does not sit in Congress, does not vote on federal laws, and does not appoint the president’s cabinet. The show puts governors in the other chair, after the House and the Senate, so the audience hears “state” before it hears a list of names.

## What we will not say

- We will not say who took the House, the Senate, or any governor.
- We will not say the president was re-elected or defeated. The president was not a candidate.
- We will not call a governor’s race a federal race.
- We will not treat the two specials as if they were ordinary Class II seats without saying “special.”
