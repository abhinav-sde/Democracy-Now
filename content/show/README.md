# Democracy This Week — the weekend show

This folder is the production book for the **1–2 hour** weekend edition. It is separate from the website. The site publishes a short brief. This book is the prime-time show that brief can be read from.

The show airs **Sunday evening**. The working weekend is the Saturday before that Sunday. A month has four or five of those weekends, and every one of them has a rundown.

## How a month is used

Count the Saturdays. That is the number of shows.

- If a vote falls on that weekend, or in the week just closed, that vote is **The ballot**.
- The weekend before a large vote is the **preview**. The structure lesson is how that office is chosen.
- A weekend with no national vote still airs. It takes one structure lesson, or India, or the United States, or a date that is still only a window.
- A fifth Saturday is not filler. It is the conditional show: a second round that may not happen, or the bridge into the next month.

## Two clocks

- **90 minutes** is the normal show.
- **120 minutes** is used when two or more countries vote in the same weekend, or when a second round and another national vote share a Sunday. The rundown says which.

## What this book will not do

- It does not name a winner. A past weekend is an archive plan of the structure, not a results reel.
- It does not give a country a score. A date means an election is scheduled or was held.
- If two sources disagree, the commission or the parliament that called the vote wins, and the rundown says so.
- A second round that the law holds only when nobody wins the first round is written as two opens. The host does not assume it is on.
