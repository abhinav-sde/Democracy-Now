---
title: The parliament chooses the government
dek: Voters elect a legislature. The head of government is whoever can command it. The term is not the same length everywhere.
---

In a parliamentary system the national question is who sits in the legislature. The head of government is the person who can command that legislature: a prime minister in the United Kingdom, India, New Zealand, Latvia, and Israel, a chancellor in Germany. Lose the house and the government goes. There is no separate ballot that keeps the government in office while the parliament changes hands.

The head of state is a different job, and countries fill it in different ways. The United Kingdom has a monarch. Latvia's Saeima elects the president. Germany and India have presidents who are not the head of government. Israel's election is the Knesset. None of these is the Brazilian or American pattern, where voters elect the executive directly.

Terms are a choice, not a law of nature. New Zealand's parliament lasts three years, so it is at the polls more often. Germany's Bundestag is elected for four years. The Indian Lok Sabha and the state assemblies run for five. The United Kingdom can go five years and can also dissolve sooner. The Czech Senate is the odd one on this desk: six-year terms, with a third of the seats elected every two years, while the government itself comes from the other house.

The October 2026 Czech vote is a Senate election. It is not, by itself, a change of government. The Latvian vote on 3 October is a change of parliament, which is where that government comes from.
