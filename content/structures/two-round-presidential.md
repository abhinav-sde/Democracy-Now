---
title: When half the votes is the rule
dek: Brazil's president has to win a majority. If nobody does, the top two vote again.
---

A Brazilian president is not elected by coming first. The winner needs more than half of the valid votes. On 4 October 2026, if no candidate has that majority, the top two meet in a second round on 25 October. The Chamber of Deputies and the Senate do not get a second round of their own. A presidential runoff does not reopen those counts.

That rule is why a first-round result can be unfinished. The election happened. The presidency may still be undecided.

France uses a two-round vote for its president as well, inside a different system: the French government still has to live with the National Assembly. The United States does not hold a national second round at all. Latvia, voting the day before Brazil, is not electing a president. It is electing a parliament, and the government is formed afterwards.

The lesson is the threshold. "Who is ahead" and "who has won" are the same fact only in systems that let a plurality finish the election.
