---
title: A president and a parliament that can disagree
dek: The president is elected by the voters. The government still has to live with the assembly.
---

A semi-presidential constitution splits the executive. Voters elect a president. The president appoints a prime minister, and that government has to be able to face the parliament. France is the clearest case on this desk: a five-year presidential term, a two-round presidential election, and a National Assembly the government must survive. When the assembly majority and the president come from different camps, both stay in office. The system expects that argument. It does not treat it as a failed election.

Guinea-Bissau and Haiti use the same broad design: a directly elected president, and a parliament the government has to reckon with. Their December 2026 dates are on the calendar. Guinea-Bissau's is still marked tentative. The design of the offices is not the same thing as a promise that the vote will be held, or that it will be free and fair.

The contrast with a parliamentary country is who voters pick. In the United Kingdom or India, voters pick the legislature and the government follows. In France, voters pick the president as well, and then the assembly can still force the government to change shape.
